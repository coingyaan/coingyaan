Arc Network Testnet Guide: Points Program, Discord Roles and Building on Circle's L1

12 min read · Monesh Kumar · May 2026

Most people stumbled on Arc Network and dismissed it as just another testnet. That was a mistake. Arc is not another copycat Layer-1 trying to compete with Ethereum on transaction speed. It is built by Circle, the company that created USDC, one of the most widely used stablecoins on the planet.

Circle went public on the NYSE in June 2025, raising 1.2 billion dollars in its IPO. Arc sits at the center of their 2026 product roadmap with over 100 institutional partners including BlackRock, Visa, Goldman Sachs, Mastercard and Amazon Web Services already testing on the network. A native token is confirmed. Mainnet is coming in 2026.

The early contributors who show up now, build things, earn roles and accumulate points are the ones who will have the clearest records when anything significant happens at launch. This guide shows you exactly how to do that.

💡 Why Arc Network Is Different From Other Testnets

  • Built by Circle the same company behind USDC which processes trillions in stablecoin volume
  • USDC as native gas no volatile gas token, fees paid in stablecoins which is a first for any major Layer-1
  • Institutional focus BlackRock, Visa, Goldman Sachs and AWS are already building on Arc testnet
  • Native token confirmed Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire publicly confirmed a native Arc token for governance and proof-of-stake
  • Not just another L1 Arc is positioned as an Economic Operating System for stablecoin-native finance including FX, tokenized assets and capital markets

📋 What This Guide Covers

1. Testnet Interactions 2. Points Program and Tier System 3. Discord Community and Roles 4. Social Layer and Building Your Presence 5. Building on Arc (The Biggest Edge) 6. How to Get Noticed by the Arc Team

1. Testnet Interactions

The Arc public testnet launched on October 28, 2025. It is a live environment where developers and community members can interact with the network before mainnet. These are fun interactions to familiarize yourself with how Arc works. Do not spend excessive time here as the team has signaled testnet activity carries minimal weight on its own.

That said, doing these steps shows baseline engagement and gets you comfortable with the Arc ecosystem before you move on to higher-value activities.

Step 1: Claim Testnet Funds from the Faucet

  • Go to faucet.circle.com
  • Connect your wallet and request testnet USDC
  • These are test tokens with no real value, used only to pay gas on the Arc testnet

Step 2: Claim Your Arc Domain

  • Go to infinityname.com/arc
  • Claim a domain name on Arc this is your onchain identity on the network
  • Choose something meaningful and professional if possible

Step 3: Make Transactions on OmniHub

  • Go to omnihub.xyz/collection/arc
  • Interact with the NFT collection there to generate onchain transaction history
  • Vary your transaction amounts and timing avoid doing the same amount at the same time every session

Step 4: Deploy a Smart Contract

  • Go to zkcodex.com/onchain/deploy
  • Deploy a simple smart contract on Arc testnet no coding experience required for basic deployments
  • This demonstrates technical curiosity and puts you in the builder category from day one
⚠ The Arc team has indicated testnet activity has minimal standalone weightage. These interactions are useful for learning the network but the real opportunity lies in the points program, Discord engagement and building.

2. Points Program and Tier System

This is where the real opportunity lies for most community members. Arc has a structured points system at community.arc.network that gives you a verifiable track record of engagement. The tiers unlock increasingly significant access to the Arc team and ecosystem.

Getting started takes five minutes and gives you an immediate 100 points just for completing your profile.

How to Get Started

  • Go to community.arc.network
  • Sign up and verify with your email address
  • Fill out your complete profile to receive 100 points instantly
  • Start collecting additional points by watching videos, reading official articles and joining Arc events

The tier system is straightforward but requires genuine sustained engagement to climb. Here is the full breakdown:

Points Required Tier Badge and Access
500 points Tier 1 Architect Badge
3,500 points Tier 2 VIP List Opportunity
15,000 points Tier 3 Speak at IRL Events
40,000 points Tier 4 Collaborate with Circle Team Directly
90,000 points Tier 5 Attend the Builder Summit

Tier 4 and Tier 5 are where things get serious. Direct collaboration with the Circle team and attendance at the Builder Summit means real visibility with the people who will shape how Arc launches. These are not rewards handed out randomly. They go to people who have demonstrated consistent, high-quality engagement over time.

Strategy tip: Do not try to game the points system with low-effort clicks. The activities that earn the most points, such as creating original content, hosting community sessions and building projects, are also the ones that get you noticed by the team. Points and visibility go together.

3. Discord Community and Roles

The Arc Discord at discord.com/invite/buildonarc is where the core community lives. Getting in and getting active here is one of the most impactful things you can do in the early phase of any project backed at this level.

How to Get In and Get Noticed Fast

  • Complete your profile on the community points platform before applying to Discord it signals you are serious
  • Read every onboarding question carefully and answer thoughtfully rushed or generic answers will slow your entry
  • Once inside, spend time reading existing conversations before posting understanding context before speaking is how experienced builders communicate
  • Ask genuine questions about Arc's technical architecture, USDC gas mechanics or institutional use cases
  • Share your testnet experience with specific observations, not just "it worked"

Available Roles

  • Creator Role handpicked directly by the Arc team for members who produce valuable original content about the network
  • Moderator Role also picked from within the community for members who show consistent, helpful and constructive presence

There is no formal checklist for earning these roles. The Arc team watches how people engage and selects from community members who stand out through the quality of their contributions over time. Consistency matters more than any single impressive post.

4. Social Layer and Building Your Presence

Your public trail of engagement with Arc on X (formerly Twitter) creates a record that the team can verify. When they search your handle, what they find matters. This is especially true for anyone aiming for creator roles, speaking opportunities or direct collaboration.

What to Share on X

  • Share official Arc updates, announcements and developer blog posts as they are published
  • Post your honest feedback on testnet projects you interact with specific, constructive observations are more valuable than generic praise
  • Document your journey publicly what you tried, what you learned, what questions came up
  • Tag @buildonarc when sharing original thoughts or experiments related to Arc
  • Engage with other Arc community members quote their posts with your perspective rather than just retweeting
What makes a good Arc post: Something specific. Not "Arc looks interesting" but "I deployed a contract on Arc testnet today and the USDC gas model feels significantly different from ETH mainnet here is why that matters for payments." Specific observations get shared, quoted and noticed.

5. Building on Arc

This is the section that separates people who get noticed from people who do not. You do not need to build something sophisticated. Even a small working project on Arc testnet puts you in a category that the vast majority of community members will never reach.

Arc is EVM-compatible, which means if you have built anything on Ethereum, Polygon or any other EVM chain, you can deploy it on Arc with minimal changes. If you are starting from scratch, the Arc documentation at docs.arc.network is well-structured and beginner-friendly.

Ideas for What to Build

  • A simple payment contract that accepts USDC directly relevant to Arc's core use case
  • A token faucet or small DeFi experiment using testnet USDC
  • A tool that reads Arc chain data and displays it even a simple dashboard counts
  • A documentation translation or a tutorial you wished existed when you started
  • An integration with an existing open-source project deployed on Arc testnet

Whatever you build, document it publicly. Write a thread on X walking through what you made, what worked, what did not and what you would do differently. Share it on Discord. Post the GitHub link. The act of building and sharing creates a trail that speaks louder than any amount of commenting or sharing existing content.

6. How to Get Noticed by the Arc Team

The Arc team is a small group managing a fast-moving network backed by one of the most watched companies in institutional crypto. They do not have time to hunt for good contributors. The people who get noticed are the ones who make it easy to be noticed.

The Approach That Actually Works

  • Be consistent, not loud. One quality post per week for three months beats ten posts in one week and then silence
  • Stack your signals. Points program progress plus Discord activity plus public posts creates a verifiable record across multiple platforms
  • Focus on quality over quantity. One insightful thread about Arc's USDC gas model is worth more than twenty generic retweets
  • Build something. Even one small working project gives you a talking point in every conversation
  • Engage with builders, not just the project. Comment on what other developers are building on Arc. The team sees community dynamics
  • Show up for events. Arc hosts AMAs, developer sessions and IRL events. Being present and asking good questions gets you remembered
The big picture: Arc is still early. The mainnet is launching sometime in 2026. The people building records right now, through consistent engagement across testnet, points, Discord and social, are positioning themselves for when this matters most. The window for being an early contributor does not stay open forever.

Arc is not trying to beat Ethereum at being everything to everyone. It is trying to become the default settlement layer for institutional stablecoin finance. That is a much more specific and arguably more achievable goal. With Circle's balance sheet, USDC distribution and institutional relationships already in place, the infrastructure exists to make it happen.

If you stay active, keep helping and build even small things, you will be in the top percentage of contributors when Arc launches for real.
