How to read the funding rate

Perpetual futures use funding to keep their price tied to spot. When funding is positive, longs pay shorts, which tells you traders are leaning long and are willing to pay to hold that exposure. When funding is negative, shorts pay longs, so the lean is bearish. The size of the rate tells you how strong the lean is.

Why crowding matters

Funding is most useful at the extremes. Very high positive funding means the long side is crowded and paying a lot to stay in, which often precedes a sharp unwind if price stalls. Very negative funding means shorts are crowded, which can set up a squeeze higher. Balanced funding near zero suggests no strong positioning either way.

The CoinGyaan approach

We pull funding from several venues, average the ones that respond and translate the number into a plain read: the bias, who is paying whom, and whether leverage looks crowded. The annualized figure shows what the current rate would cost or pay over a year if it held. The venues can change and the interpretation is ours.

Frequently asked questions

What is a funding rate?

It is a periodic payment between long and short holders of a perpetual future that keeps the contract price close to spot. Positive means longs pay shorts, negative means shorts pay longs.

Is positive funding bullish?

It reflects bullish positioning, but very high positive funding can be a caution sign because crowded longs are prone to sharp unwinds. Read it together with price and open interest.

How often does it update?

This page refreshes every 5 minutes. Venues typically settle funding every 8 hours.
